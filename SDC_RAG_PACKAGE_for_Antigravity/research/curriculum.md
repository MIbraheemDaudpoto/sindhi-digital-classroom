## 2. Full chapter list — 12 chapters, in order, with topics

Chapter titles below are as printed in the textbook scan. Topics are taken only from chapter text / Student Learning Outcomes (SLOs) actually read.

### Chapter 1 — Cellular Organization of Plants and Animals
Topics: What a cell is (basic unit of life; Robert Hooke, 1665); light microscope — parts (base, arm, tube, stage, eyepiece, objective lens, mirror, adjustment screws) and working; electron microscope (up to 500,000×); animal cell vs plant cell — cell wall (cellulose), cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus (nuclear membrane, chromosomes; human cell = 46), organelles: endoplasmic reticulum, mitochondria, chloroplasts/chlorophyll, vacuoles (one large in plant cells, many small in animal cells), centrioles; plant vs animal cell differences; unicellular organisms (bacteria, amoeba, chlamydomonas, paramecium) vs multicellular; tissues — plant: epidermal, xylem, phloem, mesophyll; animal: muscle, bone, blood, nerve, fat; organs — plant: leaf, flower, root; animal: heart, tongue, liver; organ systems — plant root system & shoot system; human digestive, respiratory, circulatory, nervous systems; hierarchy: cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic (book pp. 4–15)

### Chapter 2 — Sense Organs
Topics (SLOs: structure and function of eye, ear, skin, nose, tongue): five sense organs linked to the brain by nerves; eye — cornea, iris, pupil, lens, retina, optic nerve, eyelids/eyelashes, tear glands, how the eye focuses; ear — outer ear (pinna, ear canal), middle ear (eardrum; hammer, anvil, stirrup), inner ear (cochlea, auditory nerve); skin — outer and inner layers, receptors for pain/pressure/touch/heat/cold, sweat glands, Braille; nose — nostrils, nasal cavity, olfactory nerve, sense of smell; tongue — taste buds, sweet/salty/sour/bitter regions; smell and taste relationship.
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic (book pp. 17–24)

### Chapter 3 — Photosynthesis and Respiration in Plants
Topics: Internal structure of a leaf (upper/lower epidermis, stomata and guard cells, mesophyll, vascular bundle — xylem and phloem); photosynthesis defined (word equation: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, in sunlight/chlorophyll); importance of photosynthesis; factors affecting photosynthesis (light, temperature, carbon dioxide, water, chlorophyll); leaf structure suited to photosynthesis; respiration in plants (glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy; gas exchange through stomata; day/night); photosynthesis vs respiration comparison.
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic (book pp. 26–33)

### Chapter 4 — Environment and Interactions
Topics: Environment defined; components of environment; biotic components — producers, consumers (herbivores, carnivores, omnivores), decomposers (bacteria and fungi); dependence of organisms on one another (food, shelter, protection; plants depend on animals for carbon dioxide and pollination); food chain; abiotic components — light, temperature, air, soil, water (desert vs rainforest comparisons, adaptations); relationships among organisms — predator–prey (predation), parasitism (parasite/host; mosquito, leech, cuscuta), mutualism (lichen; termites).
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic (book pp. 35–43)

### Chapter 5 — Atoms, Molecules, Mixtures and Compounds
Topics: Matter and atoms (smallest particle taking part in a chemical reaction; protons, neutrons, electrons mentioned); molecules (oxygen O₂, water H₂O); elements — definition, symbols of common elements (table: Al, Ca, C, Cl, Cu, Au, H, I, Fe, Hg, N, O, P, Ag, S, Na); classification into metals and non-metals; physical properties and uses of metals and non-metals; alloys (brass, German silver); compounds vs mixtures; air as a mixture; separating mixtures — filtration, sublimation (ammonium chloride/noshadar, iodine, camphor, naphthalene), distillation (boiling points; distilled water, desalination, oil refining), paper chromatography (separating dyes); safety measures in science experiments. (SLOs also mention sources of carbon dioxide and maintaining its level in nature.)
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic (book pp. 45–57)

### Chapter 6 — Air
Topics (per SLOs and text read): Importance of air; atmosphere and its four layers (troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere); wind and wind vane; composition of air — nitrogen ≈78%, oxygen ≈21%, carbon dioxide ≈0.03–0.04%, rare gases, water vapour, ozone, smoke/dust; properties and uses of nitrogen (food preservation, electric bulbs, fertilizers — ammonia/urea/ammonium sulphate, dyes, medicines, explosives, liquid nitrogen as coolant); properties and uses of oxygen (respiration, burning, rusting, hospitals, divers/climbers/astronauts, welding, rockets); properties and uses of carbon dioxide (photosynthesis, soda water, fire extinguishers, baking, dry ice; limewater test); rare gases and uses (argon in bulbs/lamps, neon in advertisement lights, helium in balloons); water vapour, smog; greenhouse effect introduced.
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic/51-100 (book pp. 59–65)

### Chapter 7 — Solutions and Suspensions
Topics: Solution and its components — solute, solvent; types of solutions (solid/liquid/gas in liquid; air; alloys such as brass, bronze, steel); aqueous solutions; particle model of solution and of solids/liquids/gases; water as a universal solvent; dilute and concentrated solutions; saturated and unsaturated solutions; solubility (amount in grams per 100 g solvent) and effect of temperature on solubility (solids vs gases); suspensions — definition, examples (chalk in water, muddy water, lassi, blood, medicines, slurry), separation by filtration; solutions vs suspensions compared; uses of solutions and suspensions in daily life.
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic/51-100 (book pp. 67–76)

### Chapter 8 — Energy and Its Forms
Topics: Energy defined (ability to do work); potential energy (chemical, stored mechanical, gravitational, nuclear) and kinetic energy; other forms — heat, light, electrical, sound; conversion of energy between forms; law of conservation of energy (pendulum example; energy dissipation as heat); energy converters (lamp, TV, radio, drill, washing machine, calculator; plants as converters); renewable vs non-renewable sources — fossil fuels; hydro-electric, wind, biogas, solar, tidal energy; energy in the human body (food); energy transfer in an environment.
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic/51-100 (book pp. 78–88)

### Chapter 9 — Forces and Machines
Topics: Simple machines (lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, screw — lever/inclined plane/wedge carried over from Class 5); wheel and axle — working both ways (lifting load, increasing speed), examples (screwdriver, steering wheel, tap handle, mincing machine, hand drill, well crank); pulley — fixed pulley, moveable pulley, pulley system ("block and tackle"), uses (wells, flag poles, cranes, construction); gears — gear train, driving and driven gears, increasing speed vs increasing force; uses of gears (hand drill, bicycle gear system, wind-up clock, vehicles); designing pulley/gear systems.
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic/51-100 (book pp. 90–98)

### Chapter 10 — Properties of Light
Topics: Light as energy; luminous and non-luminous objects; transparent, translucent, opaque materials; transmission, absorption and reflection of light; reflection — incident/reflected ray, normal, angles of incidence and reflection, laws of reflection; regular (smooth surface) vs diffused (rough surface) reflection and everyday applications; images formed by a plane mirror (upright, same size, laterally inverted, virtual, image distance = object distance) and ray diagrams; pin-hole camera (Al-Haithem) and mirror vs pin-hole image comparison; uses of reflecting surfaces — periscope, reflecting telescope, microscope; multiple reflections — kaleidoscope (mirror angle vs number of images); types of mirrors — plane, concave, convex; terms (centre of curvature, pole, principal axis, principal focus); image formation by concave and convex mirrors; uses of each mirror type.
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic/51-100 (Ch. 10 start, book pp. 100–102) and https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic/101-129 (book pp. 103–111)

### Chapter 11 — Investigating Sound
Topics (SLOs: sound as a form of energy; compare speed of sound in solids, liquids, gases; materials sound travels through; how the human ear receives sound): Sound produced by vibration; sound as energy (loud = more energy, soft = less); how sound travels — needs a material medium (solid, liquid, gas), cannot travel in vacuum; speed of sound — air ≈350 m/s, water ≈1,500 m/s, steel ≈6,000 m/s (fastest in solids); light faster than sound (lightning/thunder); how the human ear receives sound (pinna → ear canal → eardrum → hammer/anvil/stirrup → cochlea → auditory nerve → brain); protecting the ears / hearing damage from loud noise.
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic/101-129 (book pp. 113–120)

### Chapter 12 — Space and Satellites
Topics (SLOs: define satellite; compare comets, asteroids, meteors; kinds of meteors; Halley's Comet; artificial and geostationary satellites; milestones in space technology; uses of satellites; GPS): Heavenly bodies and the universe; natural satellites (the Moon; planets' moons); asteroids — asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, Ceres and Vesta; comets — head, coma, tail; Halley's Comet (every 76 years, last seen 1986, Giotto probe); meteoroids, meteors ("shooting stars") and meteorites (Hoba, Arizona crater); artificial satellites — Sputnik 1 (1957), Explorer 1 (1958); orbits — geostationary, polar, eccentric, low Earth orbit; satellite receiving stations; launching satellites (multistage rockets); key milestones in space technology (1957 Sputnik-1 → 1969 Apollo 11 Moon landing → Skylab 1973 → Sally Ride 1983 → Mir 1986 → Phoenix Mars lander 2007, as printed); kinds of artificial satellites — Sputnik-I, Explorer 1, geostationary satellites (incl. Pakistan's PAKSAT-1R, 11 Aug 2011), Landsat, communication satellites, polar satellites; GPS; visiting space (Armstrong and Aldrin 1969; India's Aryabhatta 1975; Pakistan's Badr-I, 1990s).
Source: https://anyflip.com/mrpjd/qqxr/basic/101-129 (book pp. 122–131)

## 4. Gaps and failures — read before reusing this list

1. **No official STBB table of contents was found.** The official portal page for Science VI (https://portal.stbb.edu.pk/ebooks/book.php?id=122) shows only title, class, medium (Sindhi), year (2020-21) and a PDF viewer that yields no text via browser.open; downloads were prohibited by the task. The chapter list therefore rests on a scanned textbook copy (AnyFlip) plus Sindh-attributed secondary listings (Testtiari, Minideo) — strong corroboration, but not an official TOC page.
2. **Sindhi-language chapter titles were NOT obtained.** All titles above are the English titles exactly as printed/given by the sources. The Sindhi-medium book's Sindhi chapter headings were not extracted (the scanned copy read is the English text of the same 12-chapter book). Do not fabricate Sindhi translations.
3. **Edition currency:** The only edition year verified for Science VI is **2020-21** (official portal + MedicoEngineer). Whether a newer STBB edition changes the chapter list is unknown.
4. **Testtiari coverage is partial:** it carries MCQ content only for Chapters 1–8; Chapters 9–12 show "Working on this chapter, MCQs will updated soon." Titles for Chapters 9–12 are nonetheless verified from the textbook scan itself and (for 9, 10) Minideo.
5. **Excluded look-alike lists (proven NOT STBB — do not reuse):**
   - A 16-chapter list ("Natural Resources – Air, Water and Land; The Living World; … Fun with Magnets; The Universe") from a Scribd upload is the **Maharashtra (MSCERT, Pune) Standard Six General Science**, per its own sanction page — not Sindh.
   - A 12-chapter list ("Science around us; Cellular organization; Food and digestion; …") from a Scribd upload is the **federal Single National Curriculum (SNC) zero draft, Grade VI–VIII, 2020** (National Curriculum Council, Islamabad) — a curriculum framework, not the STBB textbook, and its chapter titles differ from the textbook.
   - A near-identical 12-chapter list at freebooks.pk is labelled **Punjab Board**; it agrees closely with the STBB list but was not used as a citation source for STBB claims.
